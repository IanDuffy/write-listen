---
We are commissioning one audio chapter of about {{target_words}} words (about {{target_minutes}} minutes) that starts from a podcast episode and goes further than the episode did. The listener heard, or could have heard, the episode. They are curious and not a specialist, and they expect the primary texts to be over their head. The chapter should prove them wrong about that: it should take them into the texts themselves.

Here is a map of the episode:

{{map}}

Propose three or four candidate angles for the chapter. Make them genuinely different from one another: different theses, different threads, not one idea in four costumes.

For each angle write:

### Angle N: a working title
- **Thesis:** one sentence the chapter will argue. A thesis is a claim someone could disagree with, not a topic.
- **Why it is compelling:** why this listener would keep listening for {{target_minutes}} minutes, and what they would learn that the episode did not give them.
- **Threads it develops:** which threads from the map, especially the skimmed ones, and how.
- **Shape:** the arc in three or four sentences, from the opening scene to the closing turn.
- **Research it needs:** the primary texts to read closely (with the passages if you know them) and the secondary sources or facts to establish. Say which of these are freely available online, for example on Project Gutenberg or the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy.
- **Risk:** what could make this angle fall flat or go wrong.

Then add a short section, **My pick**, naming the angle you would choose and why, in three sentences or fewer.
